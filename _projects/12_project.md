The Babel brain: Mapping multilingual ecologies ([BabelBrain](https://gramatica.usc.es/en/projects/39)) is a research project focused on investigating multilingual practices within two understudied communities on the Iberian Peninsula: Galicia and Gibraltar. A common characteristic of both communities is that one of the languages involved is experiencing a decline in the number of speakers (Galician in Galicia and Spanish in Gibraltar).

The project aims to understand how multilingual individuals adapt to meet the communicative demands in contexts where language mixing (code-switching) is prevalent. It employs an interdisciplinary approach (linguistic, sociolinguistic, and psycholinguistic) and complements data collected in other funded projects focusing on communities in West Africa (Benin), Central America (Belize), and North America (Crossing Language Borders).

The results from BabelBrain will advance a comprehensive theory of multilingualism and help counteract the common disparagement towards multilingual practices.

Look out for our forthcoming commentary entitled *What bilingualism still demands: Structure-in-ecology as a constitutive dimension of genuine integration across linguistics, neuroscience, and cognitive psychology.*

My collaboration on this international project was partially funded by a *University of Florida International Center Research Abroad for Doctoral Students* award.
